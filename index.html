About Me

JA

Building Dreams, Shaping Futures

Born and raised in Kisii, my journey into architecture began with a simple fascination: how spaces shape human experiences. From the rolling hills of my hometown to the classrooms of Kapsabet High School, I observed how environment influences behavior, culture, and opportunity.

This curiosity led me to the Technical University of Kenya (TUK), where I pursued a degree in Architecture. There, I didn't just learn to design buildings—I learned to solve problems, to listen to communities, and to envision futures where structures serve as catalysts for transformation.

Upon graduation, I founded Archabitive, driven by a singular mission: to prove that African architects can compete and excel on the global stage while remaining deeply rooted in local context and community needs.

My Journey

The Beginning - Kisii

Early Years

Growing up in Kisii, I was surrounded by organic architecture—homes built from local materials, structures that responded to climate and terrain. This early exposure taught me that great design doesn't require expensive materials; it requires understanding and respect for context.

Education - Kapsabet to TUK

2012 - 2019

At Kapsabet High School, I excelled in technical drawing and mathematics, finding joy in translating ideas into precise visual representations. This passion propelled me to TUK, where I immersed myself in architectural theory, sustainable design, and urban planning. My final year thesis explored affordable housing solutions for rapidly urbanizing communities—a theme that continues to drive my work today.

Founding Archabitive

2020

Shortly after graduating, I took a leap of faith and founded Archabitive. The name combines "architecture" with "initiative"—reflecting my belief that architects must be proactive problem-solvers, not passive service providers. With a small but talented team, we began taking on projects that others overlooked: small residential renovations, community centers, and micro-commercial spaces. Each project taught us invaluable lessons about client needs, construction realities, and business management.

Growth & Vision

2020 - Present

Today, Archabitive has grown to a team of six specialists, working across residential, commercial, and institutional sectors. But our ambition extends far beyond Kenya's borders. We're actively pursuing international collaborations, showcasing that African architectural firms can deliver world-class design while maintaining competitive pricing and deep cultural insight. Every project we complete is a statement: Africa belongs at the table of global architecture.

Design Philosophy

Context-Driven Design

Every project begins with listening—to the site, the client, and the community. I believe great architecture emerges from deep understanding of local climate, culture, materials, and aspirations. Generic solutions have no place in my practice.

Sustainable Innovation

Sustainability isn't a trend; it's a responsibility. I integrate passive design strategies, local materials, and energy-efficient systems into every project. Modern doesn't mean wasteful—it means intelligent.

Human-Centered Spaces

Buildings are for people. Whether designing a family home or a commercial complex, I prioritize how spaces will be lived in, worked in, and experienced. Aesthetics matter, but functionality and emotional resonance matter more.

Featured Projects

01
Residential Nairobi, Kenya 2023

The Riverside Residence

Challenge

Design a contemporary family home on a sloped riverside site that maximizes natural light while maintaining privacy and security.

Approach

Created a terraced design following the natural topography. Large openings capture natural light and ventilation while strategically placed walls ensure privacy. Locally sourced stone and timber honor traditional Kenyan craftsmanship.

Outcome

A warm, sustainable home that seamlessly blends indoor and outdoor living spaces, demonstrating how modern design can honor tradition.

02
Commercial Thika, Kenya 2022

Greenview Commercial Plaza

Challenge

Develop a mixed-use space that balances retail and office needs while creating an inviting pedestrian experience.

Approach

Ground floor retail with covered walkways and landscaped courtyards. Upper office spaces with natural ventilation. Integrated solar panels and rainwater harvesting systems.

Outcome

A sustainable commercial development that reduces operational costs while prioritizing human experience and environmental responsibility.

03
Institutional Kisii, Kenya 2021

Hope Primary School Extension

Challenge

Expand educational facilities within a limited budget while maintaining quality learning environments.

Approach

Collaborated with the community, using local materials and labor. Designed bright, well-ventilated classrooms with cross-ventilation and natural lighting to reduce energy costs.

Outcome

Four new classrooms and a library that prove quality educational infrastructure doesn't require exorbitant budgets—just thoughtful design and community collaboration.

04
Residential Nairobi, Kenya 2023

Urban Loft Renovation

Challenge

Transform a dated 1970s apartment into a modern urban loft while respecting the building's original character.

Approach

Removed non-structural walls and introduced mezzanine levels to maximize vertical space. Industrial finishes pay homage to the original structure while contemporary furnishings create freshness.

Outcome

A compact yet spacious modern loft that demonstrates how thoughtful renovation can breathe new life into existing structures.

Residential Kenya Work in Progress

Multi-Level Private Residence

Overview

The design organizes a multi-storey residence around vertical zoning, clear circulation cores, and layered indoor–outdoor spaces, while integrating services, staff accommodation, and site infrastructure. This project demonstrates comprehensive thinking about program complexity, vertical stacking, and systems integration at the residential scale.

Design Response

Multi-level residential planning with clear separation of public, private, service, and DSQ zones. Vertical circulation establishes spatial hierarchy across floors, with rooftop integration for solar, water tanks, and outdoor lounge space. Full site thinking addresses boundary walls, septic systems, water infrastructure, and parking organization.

Key Features

Ground floor integrates public and semi-public spaces with outdoor living, including a sunken lounge as a distinct spatial moment. First floor organizes bedrooms with careful privacy gradients and voids connecting to spaces below. Upper floor houses rooftop lounge, office, and family spaces, capitalizing on vertical positioning for views and separation.

Status

Currently in approval phase. This project showcases the ability to handle scale, manage coordination across disciplines, and understand systems beyond form—positioning it as evidence of comprehensive architectural thinking and technical maturity.
